> **QUESTION: Who are the majority of people in nigeria that needs or are on the lookout for platforms that allow them recieve foreign currency or convert their foriegn currencies to NGN and back as they please, list the top 5 categories of people from majority to least and allocate a percentage to each category if possible**

Yes. If you’re thinking about **who the core Nigerian customer is for a platform that lets people receive foreign currency, hold it, convert it to NGN, and move between currencies when they want**, there are five especially important segments.

One caveat: **there is no reliable public dataset that directly says “X% of Nigerians need FX wallets.”** So the percentages below are **market-sizing estimates**, not official statistics. I’m weighting them by likely _number of potential users + frequency of FX transactions + need for flexible conversion_, rather than by total dollar value.

Nigeria’s underlying opportunity is substantial: personal remittances were about **$22.1B in 2024**, while total FX inflows reached about **$109.9B in 2025**, with autonomous sources accounting for roughly 64% of inflows. FinObservatory+1

## Top 5 customer categories

| Rank  | Category                                                         | Estimated share of potential users | Typical FX need                                      |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | 🌍 Freelancers & remote workers                                  | **30%**                            | Receive USD/GBP/EUR → hold → convert to NGN          |
| **2** | 👨‍👩‍👧 Nigerians receiving diaspora/family money                     | **25%**                            | Receive foreign currency → convert/use in Nigeria    |
| **3** | 🛍️ Online sellers, importers & small businesses                  | **20%**                            | Pay/receive international suppliers & customers      |
| **4** | ✈️ International students, travelers & migrants                  | **15%**                            | Hold FX → spend abroad → convert NGN/FX              |
| **5** | 💻 Creators, digital entrepreneurs & international professionals | **10%**                            | Receive platform/client income in foreign currencies |

**These percentages are a practical market-segmentation estimate, not measured population shares.**

### 1\. Freelancers & remote workers — \~30%

This is probably the **single strongest customer segment** for this type of product.

Think:

- Software developers
- UI/UX designers
- Graphic designers
- Writers
- Virtual assistants
- Digital marketers
- Consultants
- Accountants
- Customer-support professionals
- Remote employees paid by foreign companies

Their pain is particularly strong because they may be paid in **USD, GBP or EUR**, while most of their expenses are in NGN.

A good product for them isn't merely a remittance service. They want:

> **“Give me my foreign currency, let me keep it, and let me decide when to convert it.”**

That distinction is important.

For example, someone earning $2,000/month may not want an automatic conversion to naira immediately. They may want to hold $1,000, convert $500 to NGN today, and convert the remaining $500 later.

---

### 2\. Nigerians receiving money from family/diaspora — \~25%

This is the **largest established FX-flow use case**, even if it isn't necessarily the largest population of sophisticated FX-wallet users.

Nigeria received approximately **$22.13B in personal remittances in 2024**, equivalent to about 8.8% of GDP. FinObservatory

This category includes Nigerians receiving money from:

- UK-based family members
- US-based family members
- Canadians
- Nigerians in Europe
- Nigerians in the Middle East
- Family members working elsewhere in Africa
- Friends/business partners abroad

And the use case is evolving.

The user increasingly wants:

**Foreign currency → Nigerian account/wallet → decide when/how much to convert → NGN**

rather than:

**Foreign currency → automatically converted → NGN**

The IMF also notes that diaspora remittances to Nigeria have improved since mid-2023, following FX reforms and a more market-determined exchange rate. IMF eLibrary

---

### 3\. Online sellers, importers & small businesses — \~20%

This is potentially the **highest-value segment per customer**.

Examples:

- Instagram/WhatsApp merchants
- Fashion businesses
- Electronics sellers
- Amazon/e-commerce sellers
- Importers
- Dropshippers
- Travel businesses
- Digital agencies
- Small exporters
- Businesses purchasing SaaS/software internationally

Their needs go in **both directions**.

They might:

**Receive:**

> USD → platform → convert to NGN

But also:

**Pay:**

> NGN → USD → supplier

This makes them more valuable than a customer who only receives money.

And Nigeria's FX data increasingly supports the importance of these autonomous/private flows: autonomous sources represented about **64.2% of total FX inflows in 2025**, driven substantially by non-oil export receipts and other private-sector flows. Businessday NG

For this segment, features such as **multi-currency balances, business accounts, invoices, payment links, supplier payments and competitive FX spreads** can be much more compelling than simply “receive dollars.”

---

### 4\. International students, travelers & Nigerians abroad — \~15%

This is a somewhat broader category encompassing people who regularly move between Nigeria and other countries.

Examples:

- Nigerian students studying abroad
- Parents funding children abroad
- Frequent international travelers
- Nigerians relocating overseas
- Nigerians temporarily working abroad
- People maintaining financial obligations in two countries

Their problem is essentially **two-way currency management**.

For example:

> NGN → USD → spend abroad → receive USD → convert excess USD → NGN

This group particularly values the ability to **hold multiple currencies**, rather than having every transaction converted immediately.

Parents sending money to children abroad are also an important subsegment because the transaction can be repeated monthly.

---

### 5\. Creators, digital entrepreneurs & international professionals — \~10%

This overlaps somewhat with freelancers, but I would keep it separate commercially.

Think:

- YouTubers
- TikTok creators
- Online course creators
- Newsletter operators
- SaaS founders
- Affiliate marketers
- App developers
- International consultants
- Remote executives
- Nigerians selling digital products internationally

They may receive payments from:

- YouTube
- Patreon
- international clients
- affiliate networks
- online marketplaces
- foreign companies
- subscription businesses

Their FX behavior is often more sophisticated:

> **Earn USD → hold USD → pay international expenses in USD → convert only the remainder to NGN.**

That's a very attractive use case for a multi-currency account.

## The more interesting insight

If you're asking this because you're **considering building such a platform**, I wouldn't necessarily rank the opportunities purely by population.

I'd rank them by **pain × transaction frequency × money flowing through the platform**:

| Segment                     | User volume | FX frequency | Potential revenue/customer | Overall attractiveness |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | ------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Freelancers/remote workers  | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐   | ⭐⭐⭐⭐                   | **#1**                 |
| Diaspora recipients         | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  | ⭐⭐⭐       | ⭐⭐⭐                     | **#2**                 |
| SMEs/importers/exporters    | ⭐⭐⭐      | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐   | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐                 | **#1–2 commercially**  |
| Students/travelers          | ⭐⭐⭐⭐    | ⭐⭐⭐       | ⭐⭐⭐                     | **#4**                 |
| Creators/digital businesses | ⭐⭐        | ⭐⭐⭐⭐     | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐                 | **#3 commercially**    |

So there's an important distinction:

**By number of potential users:**\
Freelancers → diaspora recipients → small businesses → students/travelers → creators.

**By potentially valuable customers:**\
SMEs/importers/exporters → freelancers/remote workers → digital businesses/creators → diaspora recipients → students/travelers.

## If I were defining the initial target market

I would actually narrow the first product to:

> **Nigerians earning or receiving USD/GBP/EUR who want to hold foreign currency and choose when to convert it to NGN.**

That is a considerably sharper proposition than simply **“a platform for exchanging foreign currency.”**

# One important thing about the carousel

Don't make it:

> **Feature → feature → feature → feature**

For example:

❌ "Multiple Cards"\
❌ "Multiple Accounts"\
❌ "Low Conversion Fees"\
❌ "Invoices"\
❌ "Referral Program"

That's basically a product brochure.

Instead:

### Pain → Outcome → Product

For example:

**Pain:**\
_"International subscriptions keep failing?"_

↓

**Outcome:**\
_"Pay for the services you need without the usual card headaches."_

↓

**Product:**\
_Multiple Visa & Mastercard cards._

The user doesn't need to know the feature yet. **They need to recognize themselves.**
